# Timbum

A blocky shooter made with Godot 4.7: free-for-all deathmatch, Team Deathmatch, Gun Game, King of the Block, Infected, or 25 v 25 Tactical in an office block. Play against bots offline, or host a match and have friends join from the same Wi-Fi, with a room code, through Steam, or by IP.

## Features

- **Free-for-all deathmatch.** The first player to reach the kill limit wins, or whoever has the most kills when time runs out. When the match ends, every player's camera turns to the winner, who performs their win emote. In team modes (Team Deathmatch, Infected, Tactical) each player on the winning side who is alive sees their own character, full body, doing their emote; everyone else sees the winning side's top player. Then a results screen shows the final table, match highlights (best streak, sharpshooter, survivor), your accuracy and most-used weapon, an itemised list of the coins and XP you earned, and the next map, mutator and time of day. You can pick your loadout preset for the next match and switch its primary and secondary right there, and start it early once most of the players in the lobby press Start Next Match (everyone sees how many are ready). Hold Tab to see the scoreboard over the results screen too.
- **Team Deathmatch.** Pick Team Deathmatch in match setup. Two teams, ALPHA (blue) and BRAVO (orange), on the deathmatch maps with respawns. Every kill of an enemy scores a point for your team; the first team to the kill limit (25 to 100, default 50) wins, or the higher score when time runs out (equal scores are a draw). Friendly fire is off. Humans are split evenly and bots fill both teams evenly; you spawn on your team's half of the map near teammates and away from enemies. Team colours show on armbands, nametags (teammates' tags show through walls), the kill feed and the scoreboard (grouped by team, with each team's score), and the top of the HUD shows the score race. A team win counts as a win for everyone on the winning team. Mutators work as in Deathmatch.
- **Gun Game.** Pick GUN GAME in match setup. Everyone climbs the same ladder of guns: Bit-SMG, Quad-Bit SMG, AR-Cube, Compact Carbine, Tri-Cube, Stacker LMG, Stacker Minigun, Brick Buster, Drum Buster, Block Marksman, Bolt Driver, Voxel Longshot, Boom Block, Bitstorm MP, Pocket Boomer, Tri-Tap, Cuber .44, Whisper .22 and Block 9, then the Pixel Blade (everyone carries the knife in Gun Game, whatever melee weapon their loadout holds). Every kill hands you the next gun, which you draw on the spot. A knife kill (quick melee counts) also knocks your victim down a step. The first knife kill on the last step wins; when time runs out, the highest level wins. Match setup offers a ladder of 5, 9, 14 or all 19 guns (shorter ladders skip evenly through the list). Your loadout is ignored, there are no grenades, and Rocket Party and Snipers Only are never rolled. A card in the top left shows your level, the gun you hold and the next one; the scoreboard is sorted by level. Bots fight with whatever gun they are on, and on the knife they hunt the nearest player.
- **King of the Block.** Pick King of the Block in match setup. A glowing zone sits on one of each map's 5 to 9 spots. Stand in it alone and you score a point every second; with two or more players inside it is contested and nobody scores. The zone moves to another spot every minute, with a 10-second warning that also shows where it is going. The first to the score limit (60 to 200, default 120) wins, or whoever has the most points when time runs out. The zone glows gold when free, green when it's yours, red when someone else holds it and pulses magenta when contested. A marker shows it and how far away it is through walls, the compass points to it, and a card shows who holds it, your capture progress, your points and when it moves. Bots head for the zone and fight in it.
- **Infected.** Pick INFECTED in match setup. Everyone starts as a Survivor with their own loadout. Ten seconds in, one random player turns Infected: their melee weapon only (the one in their loadout: knife, bat, axe, hammer or katana), no grenades, 150 health, 15% faster and tinted green. A Survivor who dies turns Infected and respawns as one. The Infected win as soon as nobody survives; the Survivors win if any are left when the clock runs out. Players joining after the infection started join the Infected. The HUD shows your role and the survivors left, the scoreboard shows everyone's role, and the results list who survived and who infected the most. Infected bots hunt the nearest Survivor with their melee weapon; Survivor bots shoot and back away. Rocket Party and Snipers Only are never rolled in Infected.
- **Tactical mode (25 v 25).** Pick Tactical in match setup (Play vs Bots or Host Game). 50 players, two teams of 25 (ALPHA starts at the west end of Block HQ, BRAVO at the east), bots in every slot no human takes; online, humans are split evenly between the teams. Everyone spawns with only their melee weapon, frags and their tactical grenade: guns lie in the rooms of the three-storey building (every primary and secondary, rocket launchers and snipers rare), and walking over one picks it up into its empty slot; with that slot full, press B to swap. Ammo, health and grenade crates are spread around too, and the dead drop their gun. A match opens with a 30-second planning phase behind glowing barriers: the floor plan (M) shows each storey with your teammates and the weapons, and clicks put ATTACK and HOLD markers your whole team sees in the world and on the compass. Bots move in squads of five along different routes (both stairwells, the central hallway, the atrium balconies, the alleys outside), stop and hold at each stage, and follow your markers. One life: when you die you spectate your living teammates (left/right mouse to switch) and can leave from the menu at any time. The last team standing wins (at the time limit, the team with more players alive; equal is a draw); coins and XP count a team win as victory. At the end every winning player who is still alive sees their own character perform their win emote (the others see the winning side's top survivor), then the results screen has a NEW GAME button instead of a countdown. Tactical plays slower: no grapple hook and no sliding.
- **Bots.** Easy, Normal and Hard bots navigate with a navmesh. They strafe, aim-track, throw frags and tacticals and fall back to their melee weapon (each bot carries a random one). With a sniper, crossbow or rocket launcher they switch to their other gun when you get close. They also pick up health when hurt and ammo when low, and use the same moves you do: they slide, peek around corners, go prone to snipe at long range and grapple up ledges and across gaps. Smoke blocks their sight and a flash blinds them. They aim a little sharper at whoever is running away with the match and ease off on players who are behind. Bots fill the empty slots, and one drops out each time a human joins.
- **Online play without typing an IP.** Up to 12 players; the host's machine runs the match. Position updates go out in packets small enough for any connection (under 1.1 KB each, even in 25 v 25 Tactical), so a big match doesn't stutter from lost oversized packets. Friends on the same Wi-Fi see the game listed, anyone else pastes a room code (no port forwarding), and Steam lobbies, WebRTC codes, virtual LANs and plain IP addresses work too. See [Playing online](#playing-online).
- **Pre-match lobby and map votes.** Hosting online opens a lobby before the first match, for the host and everyone who joins. It lists the players with a ready tick, level and prestige badge, the match rules the host picked, how friends can join (host), and a chat (T, as in a match). Vote for the first map among three maps of the mode plus Random (Tactical always plays Block HQ, so it has no vote); the most votes wins, and no votes or a tie play the host's map pick (or the rotation's next map). Press READY UP: the match starts as soon as everyone is ready, 10 seconds after more than half are ready (the countdown stops if that changes), or when the host presses START NOW. Anyone joining after that goes straight into the running match. The results screen after every match also has a next-map vote: the announced map and two others, with everyone's live vote counts on the buttons; the most votes picks the next map, and a tie or no votes keeps the announced one. Play vs Bots skips the lobby and starts at once.
- **Spectator mode.** Tick JOIN AS SPECTATOR on the Join screen, press SPECTATE in the lobby, or press SPECTATE in the pause menu mid-match (against bots: SPECTATE BOTS). A spectator has no body in the match (nothing to see, shoot or bump into, no pickups) and takes no player slot: a bot keeps it, and when a spectator presses PLAY in the pause menu they join like anyone joining mid-match and a bot leaves. Up to 4 spectators can watch a full 12-player match. The view follows a player through their own eyes (their gun, aim, sights, reloads and shots) with their name, health, weapon and score in the corner: left mouse or the wheel for the next player, right mouse for the previous one. N (X on a controller) switches to a free camera that flies through the map with your move keys, Space / Ctrl to go up and down and Shift to go faster; left mouse then follows the player nearest the middle of the screen. The Tab scoreboard lists the spectators under SPECTATORS. In Tactical, a dead player's view still follows only their own living team.
- **24 weapons.** Secondaries: Block 9 pistol, Cuber .44 revolver, Bitstorm MP machine pistol, Tri-Tap burst pistol, Pocket Boomer sawn-off and the suppressed Whisper .22 (no tracer, flash or flyby for anyone else). Primaries: Bit-SMG, AR-Cube rifle, Tri-Cube burst rifle, Brick Buster shotgun, Stacker LMG, Block Marksman DMR with a low-power marksman scope, Voxel Longshot sniper with a high-power scope, Boom Block rocket launcher, Compact Carbine (semi-auto, three body shots up close), Quad-Bit SMG (four-round bursts), Drum Buster (semi-auto shotgun with a drum), Stacker Minigun (150-round box, but you walk slowly and reload for 5.5 s) and Bolt Driver crossbow (marksman scope; a headshot kills at any range). Melee weapons sit in the third slot (key 3) and are what the quick melee swings: the free Pixel Blade knife, Brick Bat (wide sweeps, a little more reach), Block Axe (overhead chops), Cube Hammer (slow overhead chops and heavy steps, but one hit kills anyone at full health) and Pixel Katana (the longest reach). Each has its own model, swing and sound, and other players see the swing.
- **Grenades.** Every player carries two frag grenades, which explode the moment they touch a wall, the floor or a player, and one tactical grenade picked in the locker: a Smoke Block (a 10-second cloud) or a Flash Cube (blinds whoever is looking at it).
- **Movement.** Crouch while sprinting to slide (slopes keep it going, jump out of it to keep the speed). Peek left and right around corners; the eye, the view and your head hitbox lean out, stopping at walls. Go prone to lie flat: you crawl slowly but aim steadier (less spread and recoil) and present a much smaller target; crouch, jump or prone again gets you back up (a sprinting slide can dive straight into prone). A grapple hook reels you to whatever you aim at within 42 m. Settings > Controls > HOLD OR TOGGLE switches Sprint, Peek, Aim down sights and Crouch between Hold (the default: hold the button) and Toggle (press once, keys and controller alike): toggled sprint runs until you stop moving forward, aim, fire or press it again; toggled peek stays leaned until you press the same side again or the other side; toggled aim stays up until you press it again or sprint; toggled crouch stays down until you press it again, jump or sprint.
- **Every match plays differently.** The map rotation is shuffled, the time of day changes (day, sunset or night), and half the matches roll a mutator: Low Gravity, Turbo, Big Heads, Vampire, Rocket Party, Snipers Only or Glass Cannon. Rocket Party and Snipers Only hand everyone the Boom Block or the Voxel Longshot and keep your own primary as the backup in your secondary slot. You can switch mutators off in match setup.
- **Gunplay.** Aiming down sights, recoil, spread bloom, damage falloff, headshots, auto, semi and three-round burst fire, hitmarkers, damage numbers (rapid hits on one target add up into a single total), kill feed, kill streaks and multi-kills. Damage numbers and the kill feed can each be turned off in Settings. When you die, the camera turns to watch your killer (zooming in if they are far away, and dropping to where you fell or back to your body when a wall hides them) and the death screen tells you how far away they were, how many hits and how much damage they dealt you (shield included), and how much health (and Overshield) they had left.
- **Kill cam.** When another player (or a bot) kills you, about a second later a replay shows the last moments before your death through your killer's eyes: their view, their gun in their skin, their aim, shots, tracers and muzzle flashes, and everyone else where they were, with you labelled YOU. A KILLCAM banner names the killer and the weapon; press K (A on a controller) to skip to the death screen. The replay never delays your respawn: it ends just before you respawn, so with the usual 3-second respawn it shows about the last 2 seconds (longer respawn timers allow up to 4 seconds). Grenade, rocket and Block Bomber kills show the killer's view too; suicides, falls and Tactical (where the dead spectate their team) have no replay. It works offline and online (every player records what their own game already shows, so it costs no network traffic), pauses with an offline match, and can be switched off in Settings > Gameplay.
- **Killstreak rewards.** Kills without dying earn rewards: 3 in a row a **Radar Sweep** (every enemy shows on your compass for 8 seconds; there are no markers over enemies on screen; in team modes for your whole team), 5 an **Overshield** (+50 shield shown in cyan on your health bar, used up before your health) and 7 a **Block Bomber**: press 4 to aim it (the line of blasts shows on the ground), then fire or 4 again to call it in on that spot. Everyone sees a red ground warning for 2 seconds and hears the siren, then five explosions walk along the line; its kills are yours (with a bomb icon in the kill feed) but don't extend your streak. A card above your health shows your streak, the next reward, radar time and when an enemy radar has spotted you. Dying loses your shield, radar and an unused bomber. Bots earn and use rewards too, and run from a bomber's warning (not always in time). Match setup's STREAKS row turns them on or off; they are on by default in Deathmatch, Team Deathmatch and King of the Block, off in Gun Game, Infected and Tactical.
- **Quick ping.** In the team modes (Team Deathmatch, Infected and Tactical) press the middle mouse button to mark what you are aiming at for your team: aim at an enemy for a red ENEMY marker, or anywhere else to mark that spot with your name. Teammates (and only teammates) hear it and see the marker with its distance through walls and a pip on the compass for 6 seconds; your next ping replaces your last. Pings are limited to one every 0.6 seconds and three in five seconds. Bots call out enemies they spot the same way, and a teammate bot with nothing to shoot goes to check an enemy pinged nearby. In free-for-all modes the key does nothing.
- **Sprays.** The shop's SPRAYS tab sells 10 pixel-art sprays (GG is free; Heart, Smiley, Gold Star, Bolt, Skull, Ghost, Flame, Crown and the Timbum Cube cost 150 to 600 coins), and the locker's SPRAY row picks the one you use. In a match, press Y to paint it on the wall, floor or ceiling you are looking at within 4 m. Everyone sees it, including players who join later, and it glows faintly at night so it still shows in the dark. You have one spray up at a time (a new one replaces your last) and can spray once every 10 seconds; a new match starts with clean walls. Bots don't spray.
- **HUD.** Clean, rounded, see-through cards. Top centre: a compass (with pips for Tactical's team markers, King of the Block's zone, pings, radar contacts and airstrikes) over the clock and the race against the leader (kills, Gun Game level or King of the Block points; in team modes, a bar per team: the team score race in Team Deathmatch, survivors and Infected in Infected, players left in Tactical). Kill confirmations, multi-kills, streaks, lead changes and mode events pop in as short bold banners with an icon right under that card, never over your crosshair, and fade after a moment. Bottom left: your health as a number and a slim bar (the Overshield as a cyan bar after it) with a small movement status (sliding, peeking, grappling, blinded). Bottom right: a compact weapon card with the gun's name and fire mode, a big ammo count with the reserve, the magazine bar, pips for your three slots and small chips for frags, tactical and the grapple hook, labelled with the keys you have bound. Challenge, achievement and killstreak toasts stack in one column on the right under the kill feed. Your first match shows a one-line hint of the main keys for a few seconds; the tutorial teaches the rest.
- **8 deathmatch maps.** Village, Desert Ruins, Factory, Sky Islands, Snow Fort, Harbor Docks, Jungle Temple and City Rooftops, plus Block HQ for Tactical. Each has its own sky, fog and lighting. Health, ammo and grenade pickups are spread around every map. A taken pickup shows a faint ghost and a countdown until it comes back. On Sky Islands, Harbor Docks, Jungle Temple and City Rooftops, falling off (into the void, the water, the river gorge or the street) kills you.
- **Jump pads.** Every deathmatch map (not Block HQ or the Firing Range) has 2 to 4 glowing pads that launch you along a set arc onto a higher spot or across a gap: market awnings in Village, temple terraces and a ruin top in Desert Ruins, the grate bridge and catwalks in Factory, the outer islands in Sky Islands, the fort walkways in Snow Fort, a container stack and the crane walkway in Harbor Docks, the pyramid and the river gorge in Jungle Temple, higher roofs in City Rooftops. Each landing spot is solid ground (checked by launching a player from every pad), the arc lands in the same place under Low Gravity, and you can only nudge your course in the air. A faint dashed line flows along each pad's flight path to a ring on its landing spot, so you can see where a pad throws you. Pads glow and give off light at night, and every launch makes a sound and a burst of particles. Bots route through them too.
- **Main menu.** The TIMBUM logo top left ("Blocky arena shooter · 6 modes"); a slim top bar on the right with your level and coins over STATS, CHALLENGES, ACHIEVEMENTS, Settings and Quit; your player card bottom left (name, level, XP and the daily reward); and bottom right the LOCKER, SHOP and a big PLAY button. PLAY opens a panel above it with PLAY VS BOTS, HOST GAME, JOIN GAME, FIRING RANGE and TUTORIAL; Esc (B on a controller) or BACK closes it, and coming back from a screen puts you on the button you used. Buttons light up and click when you point at them or move to them with the keyboard or controller. CHALLENGES shows how many finished challenges are waiting to be claimed. Behind the menu, part of Snow Fort glows at sunset with drifting leaves and dust, the camera slowly swaying around your own player with a clear view past them (the spot and direction are picked so no tree or wall fills the sides of the screen): wearing your outfit and hat, holding your primary gun with its skin and charm, and doing your win emote now and then. Change your look in the locker and it changes there too.
- **Shop and locker.** The shop sorts everything into four groups with sub-tabs: WEAPONS (GUNS, MELEE, ATTACHMENTS), STYLE (SKINS, CHARMS), CHARACTER (OUTFITS, HATS) and EXTRAS (WIN EMOTES, DRAWS, SPRAYS). Each item is a rounded card that shows at a glance whether it is EQUIPPED, OWNED or what it costs, and the panel on the right turns a live 3D preview with its stats and BUY or EQUIP. Coins buy guns and melee weapons, 19 weapon skins (including camo, lava, neon, gold, diamond, galaxy, candy stripes and sunset fade, plus four animated ones: Lava Flow with molten veins crawling along the gun, Neon Pulse with waves of light racing down a black grid, Aurora with rippling northern lights and twinkling stars, and Glitch with tearing rows and flickering pixels), 14 hats (including a bandana, beret, hard hat and wizard hat), 17 outfits (including a pirate, ski suit, firefighter, chef, scuba diver, cowboy, inmate and mummy), 11 gun charms (little trinkets that dangle from every gun and swing as you move), 9 win emotes, 6 draw animations (twirl, barrel roll, drop in, sidewinder, toss and catch) and 10 sprays. Draw animations are only for looks: every one finishes in the normal switch time. The locker is where you equip your loadout (including your melee weapon and tactical grenade) and look.
- **Loadout presets.** The locker keeps three named loadout presets, each a primary, a secondary and a tactical grenade from the guns you own. Pick one with its button at the top of the locker's LOADOUT section (the one marked ACTIVE is the one you spawn with; the pickers below edit it), type a name and press RENAME. While you are dead, the three presets show at the bottom of the screen under the death card: press 1, 2 or 3 (or the mouse wheel; Y on a controller cycles them) to choose the loadout for your next life, also during the kill cam. Your current life keeps its guns; the new preset takes effect when you respawn, offline and online. The results screen's LOADOUT picker chooses the preset for the next match, and its PRIMARY and SECONDARY pickers edit that preset. Modes that hand out their own weapons ignore presets: Gun Game, Tactical and the Infected (a Survivor respawning before the infection starts uses one). A save from before presets puts its loadout in all three.
- **Weapon inspect.** Press I to look over the gun you hold for 3 seconds: it comes up and turns to show its left side, skin, charm and mastery camo, rolls over to show the top and the other side, then goes back to ready. With a Laser Sight the beam sweeps along with the barrel. The knife does its own flourish instead (the other melee weapons turn like a gun): two flips over the hand, a spin, then the blade turned toward you. Firing, aiming, reloading, switching, sprinting, quick melee, a grenade, a slide or the grapple end it at once (the shot, reload or switch still happens). Other players see it too: your third-person character lifts the gun in front of its chest and turns it from side to side, and a spectator following you sees your inspect in your view. It works with every draw animation and in every mode; you can't inspect while dead (kill cam), spectating or in photo mode. A controller has no button free for it by default; bind one in Settings > Controls.
- **Attachment upgrades.** The shop's ATTACHMENTS tab sells 10 upgrades that apply to every gun: Laser Sight (tighter hip fire and a visible laser beam that always leaves the muzzle of the gun you see: your first-person gun in normal play, your character's gun in photo mode and other third-person views, every other player's gun in their hands; it stays on and follows the barrel through reloads, weapon switches, inspects and sprinting, and is off only with a melee weapon or when dead), Recoil Grip, Bloom Stabilizer, Extended Mag, Speed Loader, Quick Optic (faster aiming), Long Barrel (damage holds up further), Light Stock (faster movement while aiming), Quick Holster (faster switching) and Grapple Motor (faster hook recharge). Each has 5 levels bought one at a time, and each level costs 1.75 times the last, so the first levels are cheap and noticeable and maxing one out takes a while. The locker's ATTACHMENTS section lists every upgrade you own with an ON/OFF switch: switched off, it plays as if you hadn't bought it (a Laser Sight switched off shows no beam) and the shop says so; the bought level stays, and switching back on restores it at your next match.
- **Progression.** XP, levels, a daily reward and lifetime stats. **Stats > Export Progress** saves everything (coins, items, upgrades, stats, settings, key bindings, loadout presets and match setup presets) to a file, and **Import Progress** restores it, on the same computer or another one.
- **Match setup.** Play vs Bots and Host Game fit on one 720p screen: big mode cards on the left (each with its emblem and a one-line summary), map cards with a picture of each map in the middle (Rotation plays every map in turn; Tactical always plays Block HQ), and on the right the presets strip, only the rules that apply to the chosen mode, and START. **Presets:** both screens share up to 8 named presets. Press EDIT, type a name and press SAVE to store everything the screen is set to: mode, map, player count, bot difficulty, each mode's limit (kill limit, team kills, Gun Game ladder, King of the Block points), time limit, mutators, killstreaks and the connection. Saving under a name that already exists replaces that preset. Pick a preset in the list to see what it holds, then LOAD it, or under EDIT RENAME it to the typed name or DELETE it (press twice). QUICK PLAY (QUICK HOST on the Host Game screen) loads the picked preset and starts straight away. Presets are kept with your profile; a setting a newer version no longer offers falls back to its default when the preset loads.
- **Per-weapon stats.** Stats has a table with your kills, accuracy, headshot share of kills and damage for every weapon you have used, plus frag grenades and the Block Bomber (whose kills count for them, never for the gun you were holding). A shot is one round: one trigger pull, each round of a burst (a three-round burst is three shots), one shotgun shell however many pellets hit, one rocket, one knife swing. A hit is a shot that struck at least one enemy, so accuracy never goes over 100%. Stats are kept in every mode, offline and online.
- **Weapon mastery.** 25, 75 and 150 kills with a weapon earn its Bronze (250 coins), Silver (500) and Gold (1000) mastery, each paid once and listed on the results screen. Gold unlocks that gun's mastery camo: black scales with glowing gold veins and an accent colour of the gun's own. Switch it on per gun with the CAMO switch in the locker's MASTERY section; everyone in the match sees it on that gun, in first and third person, and on the gun when you drop it in Tactical. The locker and the shop's weapon details show each gun's mastery progress.
- **Prestige.** XP stops at level 50, and the PRESTIGE button in Stats unlocks: after you confirm, your level goes back to 1 and your prestige rank goes up by one (up to 10), with a bonus of 500 coins times the new rank. Coins, items, upgrades and stats stay. Your rank shows as a badge (★3) before your name on the scoreboard, the kill feed and your nametag, for everyone. A save from before the cap with a level above 50 keeps that level; it earns no more XP and can prestige straight away.
- **Daily and weekly challenges.** PROGRESS > CHALLENGES on the main menu lists 3 daily challenges (new ones at local midnight) and 2 weekly ones (new ones every Monday), for example "Get 9 kills with the Bit-SMG", "Slide-kill 2 players", "Win a match on Snow Fort" or "Get 60 kills in Team Deathmatch". They mix guns you own, movement (slide, prone, grapple and mid-air kills), long shots, knife and frag kills, streaks, modes and maps, and every one can be done offline against bots. The set comes from the date, so it stays the same all day (or week). Progress counts at the end of each match, online or offline, and a card pops up in the match the moment one is done. Claim each for coins and XP (daily 150 coins + 400 XP, weekly 600 coins + 1500 XP); the PROGRESS and CHALLENGES buttons on the main menu show how many are waiting. A finished challenge you forget to claim is paid automatically when its day or week ends, so you never lose it.
- **Achievements and medals.** PROGRESS > ACHIEVEMENTS on the main menu lists 29 achievements, from First Blood and 5- and 10-kill streaks to 100 headshot kills, a kill with every gun, a win on every deathmatch map, a win in every mode (and one for each mode), slide, grapple, prone and mid-air kills, long shots, knife and frag kills, a Block Bomber kill, Gold mastery and 10 finished challenges. Each shows whether it is unlocked and when, or your progress toward it, and a card pops up in the match the moment you earn it. Your stats from before this version count: anything they already reach unlocks straight away. The results screen's match highlights are medals (Best Streak, Sharpshooter, Survivor, plus Team MVP, Blade Finish, Setback Artist, Still Standing, Patient Zero and Zone Keeper in their modes), and the Achievements screen shows how many of each you have earned.
- **Art generated by code.** Textures and models are all procedural; the only image files are the app icon, which is a render of the game itself. The sound effects are 68 WAV files made in SoundCraft (`audio/sfx/`; `sfx_pack/` has the script that builds them), with several takes for footsteps, bullet impacts, knife hits and grenade bounces so repeats vary. The music is five songs that play one after another without stopping: the menus start with Blocksmith Daydream and the shop with Coin Shop Spark, then both carry on through all five songs in a shuffled order; matches play Arena Rush, Combat Groove and Arcade Arena song to song. The short results fanfare is a procedural chiptune. The block shader adds pixel-art relief, glossy metal, ice and water, swaying leaves, and a colour grade with sharpening (Settings > Video can turn the grade off).
- **Graphics settings.** Settings > Video has anti-aliasing (off, FXAA, MSAA 2x/4x or TAA), render scale (relative to a 1600 x 900 pixel budget: in a bigger window, such as fullscreen on a Retina display, the 3D view is drawn at up to that many pixels and sharpened up to full size with FSR, while the HUD and menus stay full resolution; fullscreen 2560 x 1440 went from 38 to 56 fps on the test Mac), and separate toggles for shadows, ambient occlusion, bloom, reflections, the block shader effects (relief, swaying leaves, rippling water) and the colour grade. They all apply instantly. Picking a graphics quality preset switches these to that preset's values.
- **Controller support.** Any controller Godot recognises (Xbox, PlayStation, Switch Pro and most others) plays the whole game with analog movement and look. Settings > Controls has stick look sensitivity, invert, deadzone, a response curve (Linear, Classic or Precise) and aim assist (Off, Low or Standard): near an enemy the right stick turns slower and the view follows a moving target a little. Aim assist only works offline against bots; online it is always off, so a controller never has an edge over a mouse. Every menu works with the controller too (D-pad or left stick to move, A to select, B to go back, the right stick scrolls long lists), and the on-screen key hints show controller buttons whenever the controller was the last thing you touched. Placing Tactical's floor-plan markers still needs the mouse.
- **Colourblind modes.** Settings > Accessibility > Colour mode (Off, Deuteranopia, Protanopia or Tritanopia) remaps every colour that tells sides and states apart: team colours, enemy and teammate nametags, the kill feed, hitmarkers and damage numbers, the health and shield bar, compass pips, King of the Block's zone, pings, radar contacts and airstrike warnings. A preview shows the palette. Teams also carry a shape (▲ ALPHA, ◼ BRAVO) beside their names and every player's name in the kill feed, scoreboard, nametags and HUD, a kill's hitmarker is bigger than a hit's, and the zone marker says whether the zone is free, yours, taken or contested, so colour is never the only cue. The change applies at once, even mid-match.
- **Crosshair editor.** Settings > Crosshair designs your crosshair: style (cross, dot, circle or T), size, gap, thickness, outline, centre dot, dynamic spread on or off, colour and opacity, with a live preview beside the options that shows it at twice its size over a light and a dark background, blooming as if firing. With dynamic spread on, the gap opens to match where your shots can land (moving, jumping, firing) as before; off, it stays where you set it. Aiming down sights hides it and sniper scopes draw their own reticle, as before. The defaults are the original crosshair. Changes apply at once, also from the pause menu mid-match, and are saved with your profile (and in Export / Import).
- **Photo mode.** In a match against bots, press P (Share on a controller) or PHOTO MODE in the pause menu. The match freezes, the HUD and nametags hide (your own player shows), and a free camera flies anywhere: move keys, Space / Ctrl up and down, Shift faster, Q / E to roll, hold the right mouse button to look, the wheel to zoom. The panel sets the field of view, camera speed and roll and a filter (none, colour grade, black and white, sepia). TAKE PHOTO (or Enter) saves the view without the panel as `screenshots/timbum_<date>_<time>.png` in the save folder, shows the full path, and OPEN FOLDER opens it. Esc (B) goes back to the pause menu with your view as it was. Online matches can't freeze, so photo mode is offline only.
- **Pause when you look away.** Clicking or alt-tabbing out of the game, or minimising its window, during a match, the Firing Range, the tutorial, a kill cam, the death screen or while spectating opens the pause menu (against bots it freezes the match, its sounds included). The pause menu always draws on top of everything else on screen, airstrike warnings and toasts included. Settings > Audio > Mute when unfocused (on by default) silences the game while its window isn't in front, in menus too.
- **Firing Range.** PLAY > FIRING RANGE on the main menu opens an offline practice field: four lanes with signs at 10, 25, 50 and 75 m, each with a standing dummy and one that strafes from side to side, plus a cover lane where a dummy crouches and peeks out from behind a low wall 20 m away. Dummies take damage like players (hitmarkers, damage numbers, headshots), show their health over their heads and stand up again on their spot a second after you drop them. A readout card on the right shows the gun you hold and its falloff, your last hit (damage, headshot, distance), your DPS over the last burst, how many shots and how long it takes that gun to kill at that distance (body and headshots, with your Long Barrel), and your hit rate and headshots this session. At the weapon console behind the firing line press B (X on a controller) to equip any gun or melee weapon, including ones you don't own yet (marked TRY), with your own attachment upgrades, or a frag refill, either tactical grenade or a ready grapple hook; RESET READOUT (also in the pause menu) clears the numbers. Ammo and grenades top up on their own. Nothing in the range earns coins or XP or counts toward stats, challenges or achievements. The pause menu's LEAVE goes back to the main menu, and photo mode works there too.
- **The Dev Pack and Credits.** Stats > CREDITS lists who made Timbum (Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Haiku 5.5, GLM 3.2 Flash and the developer). The Dev Pack is a four-piece set you can't buy: the Dev Suit (matte black with neon green seams, X eyes and a stitched smile; the only animated outfit, with crawling lines, pulses and a beating core), the Dev Circuit gun skin (neon circuit traces that slide along the gun), the Dev Halo hat and the Dev Core charm. The shop shows them on neon-edged cards marked CODE ONLY, and the locker gives a picker holding one a neon edge. A secret code typed on the keypad behind the DEV row of the credits (on-screen keys, the keyboard or a controller) unlocks all four for free. When the wearer gets a kill, every piece flashes white-hot and floods green, seen by everyone and in the wearer's own first-person view. Bots never wear it.

## Controls

Every action except pause can be rebound in **Settings > Controls**: two keys or mouse buttons per action, plus one controller button, stick direction or trigger. The defaults:

| Action | Key | Controller |
|---|---|---|
| Move / Look | WASD or arrow keys / Mouse | Left stick / Right stick |
| Jump / Sprint / Crouch (hold, or press once in Toggle mode) | Space / Shift / Ctrl or C | A / L3 / B |
| Prone (lie down / get up) | Z | D-pad down |
| Slide | Crouch while sprinting | B while sprinting |
| Peek left / right (hold, or press once in Toggle mode) | Q / E | (none; bind one in Settings) |
| Fire / Aim down sights (hold, or press once in Toggle mode) | Left mouse / Right mouse | RT / LT |
| Reload | R | X |
| Primary / Secondary / Melee weapon | 1 / 2 / 3 or mouse wheel, X swaps to the last weapon | Y cycles through them |
| Loadout preset for your next life (while dead) | The same 1 / 2 / 3 or mouse wheel | Y cycles them |
| Inspect weapon | I | (none; bind one in Settings) |
| Spray | Y | (none; bind one in Settings) |
| Frag grenade / Tactical grenade | G / H | RB / LB |
| Grapple hook | F (again, or jump, to let go) | D-pad up |
| Call in Block Bomber (killstreak) | 4 (aim, then fire or 4 again; right mouse cancels) | D-pad right (then RT or D-pad right; LT cancels) |
| Ping (team modes) | Middle mouse | D-pad left |
| Swap weapon / floor plan (Tactical) | B / M | X / D-pad up |
| Weapon console (Firing Range) | B | X |
| Quick melee | V | R3 |
| Scoreboard | Tab (hold) | Back / View (hold) |
| Chat | T or Enter | (none) |
| Skip kill cam | K | A |
| Spectating: free camera / follow a player | N | X |
| Photo mode (matches against bots) | P | Share (camera: sticks, RT / LT up / down, LB / RB roll, D-pad zoom and speed, A photo, X filter, Y folder, B back) |
| Pause / settings | Esc (pauses the match when playing against bots) | Start |

Buttons are named the Xbox way; on a PlayStation pad A is Cross, B Circle, X Square and Y Triangle. A controller has fewer buttons than there are actions, so the direct weapon slots, last/previous weapon, peeking, chat, weapon inspect and spray have no controller default. In Tactical, X both reloads and swaps weapons at a weapon spot (the gun you pick up comes loaded) and D-pad up opens the floor plan (Tactical has no grapple hook). A both jumps and skips the kill cam (which only plays while you are dead).

## Playing online

**Host:** choose **Host Game**, pick your map and settings and a **Connection**, then press Start Hosting. You and everyone who joins wait in the lobby, which shows how friends can join (room code with a COPY button, your addresses, or a Steam invite button); once the match is running, the pause menu (Esc) shows the same.

**Join:** choose **Join Game**, then whichever works:

| Way to join | Works across | What the joiner does | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nearby games** | Same Wi-Fi / LAN | Click the game in the list | Nothing; hosts answer UDP discovery on port 47777 |
| **Room code** (Connection: Code + Wi-Fi) | The internet | Paste the code, press Join | Internet access. The code server ([noray](https://github.com/foxssake/noray) at `tomfol.io`) tells both sides each other's address so they connect directly; if the routers block that, it relays the game traffic. Codes are case-sensitive, so use COPY and PASTE. |
| **Steam** (Connection: Steam) | The internet | Accept the Steam invite, use **Find Steam Games**, or paste the lobby number | Steam open and signed in on both computers. Uses Valve's free test app (Spacewar, app id 480), so Steam shows you as playing Spacewar. |
| **WebRTC code** (Connection: WebRTC) | The internet | Paste the `W-XXXXXX` code | Internet access; peers meet through the free [PeerJS](https://peerjs.com) server. A fallback when room codes fail; no relay, so some strict networks can't connect. |
| **Address** | LAN, virtual LAN, or the internet | Type an IP or hostname and the port (default 7777) | For a virtual LAN, both players join the same [Tailscale](https://tailscale.com) or [ZeroTier](https://www.zerotier.com) network and use the address the host screen labels TAILSCALE or ZEROTIER. For a [playit.gg](https://playit.gg) tunnel, use the address it gives you. A public IP needs UDP 7777 forwarded on the host's router. |

The host's firewall must allow Timbum. Steam and WebRTC support are native add-ons for macOS, Windows (64-bit) and Linux (x86_64); see `addons/`.

## Running from source

1. Install [Godot 4.7](https://godotengine.org/download).
2. Open `project.godot`, or run `godot --path .`

## Building release versions

You need the Godot 4.7 export templates installed. Run:

```sh
godot --headless --path . --export-release "macOS"   build/macos/Timbum.zip
godot --headless --path . --export-release "Windows" build/windows/Timbum.exe
godot --headless --path . --export-release "Linux"   build/linux/Timbum.x86_64
```

The macOS build is signed ad hoc and not notarized. The first time, open it with right-click → Open.
